The Paradox of Happiness

17 JUNE 2020 • Sachit Deshmukh • 10 min read

Stop reading. Ponder it. Put this book aside now - and before you read on, spend five minutes or five hours answering the question for yourself. Write your answers down. Make a list.

It is not very often that I have this feeling that I belong somewhere, especially not when I start to measure my worth. What makes me "me"? What is unique about me? What do I offer on the plate? All these questions keep lingering in my head, constantly nagging at me. It is not the curiosity that troubles, but the lack of any immediate answer that leads to a series of events, consisting of my spiraling emotions, and degrading thoughts, always ending with the one conclusion that I loathe - Nothing. I am a nobody. I have no interests. I do not have a passion for anything. I am living merely because I was born.

“Every morning the sun rises and takes over the sky. The blazing hot waves create a dryness, not just in the physical world, but also in the mind. An irritation, unfortunately, paired with the scorching heat, that has no apparent solution. Just having to wait for it to end. With every passing second, the heat increases, almost causing suffocation. The mind starts to take small steps aiming at some transient relief. But the trivial pleasantries do not make up for the distress the mind experiences due to the dire need to quench an eternal thirst. And it seems as though, there won't be a panacea for this burning sensation.”

Following this conclusion come the dark thoughts of giving up, giving up on life, on friends, on every single insignificant thing. Often times, getting out of this dark pit of thoughts is not very easy. It takes a great deal of being okay with having these thoughts. It takes time and effort to develop some hope, hope that something might come out of the blue, and that might be a better answer to all the questions. When I do get out, my body seems to employ a speculative eye, trying to look for any drop of passion in each and every action I do. Could it be streaming games? Could it be being a professor? Could it be having a Psychology majors? I keep thinking, without actually confirming any of the possibilities. The scavenger hunt often ends up creating a cognitive classification of activities - those I like-like and those I don't. The hunt does not end with a treasure chest, waiting for me to unravel the purpose of my life. It leads to another dark cave, devoid of any hope, exhausting me. Though sans any interest, the hunt still goes on, it never ends. The momentary unrest it causes gets worse each time. It feeds the augmenting burning rage inside me.

Start a new book. I have what I like to call my dream book. It’s a regular journal with blank pages, and I fill those pages with my hopes and dreams and words and ideas that inspire me.

The unceasing need creates a void within me. I have so far not able to fill that void. Though to be fair, there have been times I have felt that something could fill it. During the times that I feel that I am enjoying it, my mind immediately tries to amplify the surge of happiness I feel. I start to enjoy the action more. I start to think about it more often. I try to do it on a regular basis. Each time I try to find that same happiness I felt I did it the first time. That happiness is more due to the fact that I am doing the action, probably after a long while than what action I am actually doing. It all comes to me, a bit too late, by when my body just does the action out of habit, and no more out of interest.

"At one point, the distress becomes so powerful that the mere thought of being alive troubles. Absolutely nothing seems to lessen the unrest the mind feels as the body is constantly covered in layers of sweat. The heat creates a sensation of being cooked through and through. And when this irritation reaches its peak, a sudden numbness is felt. As if the body is supposed to be this unstable. As if this torture is what everyone has to go through. And the mind accepts its fate. It becomes ready to live in this hell of a life. Fortunately, after this acceptance, somehow, the mind starts to enjoy the petty little things. It does not give away its numbness, not for long, but for a very ephemeral moment. It's weird how the mind functions in such times."

The body gets used to this mechanical mannerism. And so many such things ebb and flow in my life, giving me a momentary sense of relief, only to deepen the sense of being aloof and alien as life takes me ahead in its journey. The more I try to explore, the more severe gets the longing to fill that void. With each new thing, my mind proves to me how that particular thing is not meant for me. It keeps that void empty, and yet keeps looking for something that perfects fits in.

Whatever you wrote on the list, chances are those are the things, places, people, and experiences that you believe will make you happy.  On one hand, you want to be happy, on the other, you know the things that make you happy. But you don’t do those things. Why?

Sometimes, very rarely, there’s an indifference I feel to all these thoughts. Many times the reason being my busy life. I have things to do, I think. I have a job to attend, I tell myself.  Seldom, this indifference prevails during a time when there is nothing to really do. When it does, all the interest and curiosity drains out of me. Even the things I like to do seem tiresome. I feel as if there is no possible way that I can create a perfect atmosphere to be motivated to do something. There is no point in trying, I sometimes feel.

“And yet, one fine day, the cold winds take away the summer heat and the chilly air sets in. The clouds burst into rainfall, covering each and every ally where the sun once shone. The night sky sings to the people looking at it in awe as through the clouds marches every spark of the lighting, electrifying the air around it. The cold winds gleefully dance with the rejoicing people, placing a smile on each face.”

It amazes me, how things fall into place effortlessly. One cold monsoon breeze brushes past me and I feel content. Almost too quickly. Before even I have the time to process the weather change, my mind already starts to crave a novel to read, to sip some hot chocolate, sit on the windowpane, wet one side of my body, then the other, immersing myself fully in the freezing raindrops. I can smell the onion bhajis getting fried in the kitchen, and my mouth starts to water. It is a very funny feeling, I think. I just seem to enjoy the very feel of a novel in my hands as the winds circle at my cheeks. The novel in my hand, however interesting it may get, my mind seems to savor the very moment I am in, completely or partially but voluntarily ignoring the work of fiction before me. I feel my mind dancing with the monsoon rains. I feel like a smile could cross my face. Something tells me that smile will never end. Within me, happiness seems to have reached its peak, and yet it feels not enough. It feels as though with every raindrop that falls from the window on my hand, the pleasure reaches greater heights. And it does not stop. With the soaring joy, I feel as if I am floating with the winds, engrossed in my own world, aloof yet having a sense of belonging. Every little thing just seems too perfect to be true. The gaiety seems unreal. And yet, there is solace in knowing that there is a possibility. That this is what it is supposed to feel when it comes to you. That this is what is realizing yourself feels like. And nothing can take that feeling away from you.

The answer is very simple. You are too busy. Too busy doing what? Too busy trying to be happy. This is the paradox of happiness.

— All quotes from The Rhythm of Life, by Matthew Kelly.
