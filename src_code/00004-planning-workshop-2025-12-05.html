Reflections from Planning a Classroom Pilot with Social Impact Organizations

What were my personal learnings from a planning workshop on piloting an evidence-based math intervention for eventual scale-up?

05 DECEMBER 2025 • Sachit Deshmukh • 10 min read

With strong guidance and support from the ASPIRE team at J-PAL South Asia, my colleague, Rijul Grover, and I hosted an immersive, hands-on planning workshop last week for seven organizations that are working closely with the evidence and implementation insights behind Every Child Counts.

Back in 2023, the team experimented with a new way of engaging organizations in the education space.

The idea was straightforward:

Engage a first edition of a cohort of organizations. The organizations would implement the evidence-based math curriculum in their classrooms and explore whether it could be scaled to address foundational learning gaps.

Designing this engagement took months of thinking — clarifying learning goals, structuring the journey ahead, and reflecting on how this work would deepen ASPIRE's understanding of scaling evidence. This first edition generated valuable learnings on what enables the successful completion of scale-up pilots.

Aiming to gather deeper insights into what works for scaling, I got the opportunity to explore what the landscape for the second edition of this course could look like. After multiple discussions and conversations with organizations in the education ecosystem, the team set out to answer the following question:

"Can we generate context-specific insights on what it would take to scale the Every Child Counts evidence across various states in India at an accelerated pace?"

The approach to gathering these learnings was as unique and ambitious as this question sounds. We analyzed the landscape to identify scale-up opportunities; we identified the core knowledge that organizations should hold to meaningfully align with the state education landscape, and we developed diagnostic tools to help partners explore the maturity of key enablers for scale-up in their contexts.

On 26th and 27th November, we hosted an in-person workshop with the seven organizations.

Starting September 2025, seven partner organizations engaged deeply with this knowledge transfer, completed the diagnostic exercises, and eventually brought everything together during last week's workshop. Each organization developed a clear pilot plan, articulated its learning goals, and built a model of change tailored to scaling the program in its context.

What were my personal takeaways from this workshop?

The common goal across the table was improving learning outcomes through integration.

This may sound obvious, but it stood out how each organization consistently anchors its work to improve learning outcomes. While each organization has its own programs that aid in this larger goal, a common mode of achieving that across the table was deep engagement with the state governments. When I asked the participants about the earliest signs of success, many responses reflected this focus on the involvement of the state governments.

Deep contextual knowledge aids the learning process.

Every organization demonstrated a strong understanding of what works — and what doesn't — in its geography. This clarity not only helps them think through the building blocks of scaling Envery Child Counts but also helps me to remain open to learning from the diversity of classroom contexts during the upcoming pilots.

Think boldly first, then map the pathway.

We encouraged each partner to imagine where this evidence fits into their everyday operations:

  • Would it complement their existing approach to improving learning outcomes?
  • What would it take to reach their intended goals by absorbing this evidence backed intervention?

Rijul, my colleagues, and I got the opportunity to steer the conversations on outlining the milestones and thinking intentionally about the road ahead for this intervention. It was particularly interesting to hear from Swetha Guhan, co-founder of Key Education Foundation, about her considerations as the project started to move from the 10-school pilot in the first edition to an at-scale pilot across 100+ Karnataka schools in 2024-25.

Innovate ambitiously, but execute with discipline.

A play-based curriculum such as Every Child Counts naturally sparks new ideas — especially with the growing emphasis on play-based pedagogy for FLN across the nation. While the primary and long-term ambition is to reach all students, the intended impact depends on maintaining fidelity to the evidence. Experiences and learnings from my colleagues Dr. Harini Kannan, principal investigator on the program, and Parikrama Chowdhry, Associate Director - Policy (Scale Ups) from ASPIRE at J-PAL South Asia, helped partners understand what fidelity looks like — and how innovations can stay true to it.

As we now move towards launching the pilots, I'm personally excited to learn how this evidence can be best situated within the unique contexts of each geography.
