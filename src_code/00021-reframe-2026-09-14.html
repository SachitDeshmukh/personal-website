Attending Breakthrough's ReFrame 2026

Moving into a senior role and expanding my horizons to explore the gender equity space.

14 SEPTEMBER 2026 • Sachit Deshmukh • 5 min read

I had a wonderful time at Breakthrough's ReFrame 2026 last week!

ReFrame 2026 brought together practitioners, decision makers, funders, and torch bearers in the journey to a gender equitable India to talk about gender equity in today's workforce. It was very exciting to talk to people to understand their work and make connections in a space that talks about the pervasive nature of gender-based inequality.

I spent a lot of my time during my bachelors degree interacting with my friends, peers, and professors to share opinions and lived experiences around gender, sexuality, discrimination, and inequality. In the two days that I attended ReFrame, I saw a lot of those thoughts and conversations put into action! And it felt good to see like-minded people presenting their efforts to tackle the challenges.

Various panel discussions, presentations, and workshops provided a space for everybody to share what is working, and what is not working, to ensure that gender inequality is addressed.

Some key points and conversations that stood out to me:

  • Dr. Rashmi Singh's directive on working with governments to leverage existing systems and identifying enablers to work at scale.
  • Dr. Dibyasree Ganguly's data points that highlighted gender as a mechanism of inequality that manifests into all aspects of life including expectations and aspirations.
  • Aliva Das' suggestion on shifting the perspective on gender from a thematic area to a lived reality where intersections of caste, class, and religion add to the complexity of the lived experience.
  • Dr. Shilpa Phadke's experience of how infrastructure transforms ideology, and how this philosophy is important in the context of gender inequality.

The conversations started with what is enabling the girl child to continue with education in public schools. Mobility of stepping out of the house, safely reaching school, opportunity to engage in the classroom, and safely reaching back home are critical considerations.

Enabling the adolescent girls to then pursue higher education and further employment came up as the next challenge. Organizations like Transform Rural India and I-Dream-Career shared about their career education programs.

Solutions ranging from preventing sexual harassment in work places, building self defence skills, to working towards attitudes and behaviors of men and young boys were also delibrated.

Dr. Kalpana Viswanath raised a point close to my work -

How do we avoid working in silos and bring together all the good work to collectively tackle gender inequality? How do we make concrete efforts that combine and amplify the impact of the work across the board?

I came to the event with knowledge around Breakthrough's Taaro Ki Toli curriculum. J-PAL has evaluated the intervention, and has been collaborating with Breakthrough to take that curriculum to various states. During the event, I spoke with a few organizations collaborating with Breakthrough to deepen my understanding of collaborative work.

As I move from an associate to a senior associate role at J-PAL South Asia, I am excited to dive deeper into this space, to explore how practitioners can collaborate to collectively work towards the common mission. Wish me luck and stay in touch!
