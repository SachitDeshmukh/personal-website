The Blue Apple

07 OCTOBER 2020 • Sachit Deshmukh • Starting your timer

FJNK1982-0728-2136-0012-0001 is inscribed on the disk in front of your eyes. It shines in the tube light. This is the disk. The disk would give you answers. One click, one second, and that would be it.

2136. 28 July. Twelve research-years ago, you were born. Born in a world of innovation, technology, advancement. A world of the future. You were destined to be born in this world. The twinkle in your eyes, the curiosity you had, exploring the small things lying about you, you were meant to be. You could not be more perfect for the life you were about to live in this futuristic world. You were going to undoubtedly fulfill the purpose of your birth.

You never met the individual who gave birth to you. Not until you were old enough did it occur to you one night that the process of birth too had advanced, and that the product need not be a result of any sexual activity between two individuals. You never came to terms with being factory-made. You believed you were as functioning as any other individual born to two.

The fact did not stop you from exploring the world around you. You came to know that in this world “age” was a dead concept, instead, individuals were measured in research-years. It was then that you realized that you were only six research-years. Six research-years would mean that there had been six major technological discoveries since the time you were born.

Each discovery gave you the abilities that you had been wanting. Everything around you felt like it was a part of a well-designed experiment. It had been quite easy to find out about these discoveries. One disk, one second, and you came to know everything that was to know about each one.

That's how you found out how you lived in a home without any other individual taking care. How you knew language without ever actually listening to it. How you felt your thoughts without saying them out loud. That had been the longest of the research-year you ever lived. Before that skill, you talked to yourself to the mirror hanging on the wall against your bed. The sun's reflection looked rather small in the mirror, not much different from the view through the window.

The general knowledge skill-disk seemed to confuse you more than educating. When the depth perception skill-disk was finally made, things fell into perspective. But it came with its drawbacks. You became afraid of dark passages, of distant tiny things, of illusions. It gave you a better understanding of painting life-like objects, a skill you had once installed. Your living room wall saw you putting that skill to use.

You started searching for disks which study the eye. How does the eye function, how does it perceive, how does it capture light, how does it interpret light, how does it register colors, how does the brain form images? You found the answers to these questions quite easily. But there was this one question that had not been answered yet.

What is a color? This question kept you awake for countless days, degrading your system, eating your brain. You had never experienced color. Everything around was either black or white. For you, the red of an apple looked just like the blue of any berry. The green of the leaves was equally bright as the yellow of the sun.

These were just names to you. In four research-years you found out every possible piece of information regarding color. You found out about wavelengths of light, about color-receptive cells in eyes, about things having particular colors. You knew every possible fact that was to be known about color perception, but one thing. The mere experience of color itself.

Today, after twelve research-years, finally, the day has come, when the disk regarding color perception has been produced. And right now it is right before you. You put it in the back of your head as you start refreshing your system. The disk enters your body and starts doing its function. Targeting certain areas of the brain, making changes, developing a few things, and before you know, it is done.

As you boot yourself, the first thing that pops up in front of your eyes is an image of an apple. The apple does not seem to have one consistent color, Rather it has some big and small strips alternatingly on it.

You can see color. You cannot see color.
