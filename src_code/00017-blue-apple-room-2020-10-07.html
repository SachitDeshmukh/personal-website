The Blue Apple

You choose to enter your room.

07 OCTOBER 2020 • Sachit Deshmukh • 5 mins left

Defects. You would be able to fix any defects. You enter the room, And just like the voice had warned, the door slams shut, merging with the wall like it was earlier. You try pressing at the same spot a few more times, but it does not seem to open.

You press around the door, but it does not help. This is it. This is the room where it all ends, you realize. You turn back to look at your room. Something seems different.

No object seems out of place, but the place seems to have changed. You hope you have entered your own room. All this has exhausted every bit of you. So you make a move for your bedroom, planning to rest for a while, perhaps for the rest of the day, and start afresh tomorrow.

You pass the only couch in the living room and are about to take a left for your room when you come across a wall. Although most certain that the door was right after the couch, you consider that probability of you being forgetful.

You turn back to face the living room and scan the room for your bedroom door. That's when it hits you. There are no doors here. Your room has become one single room with walls on all four sides, having no other room. It has turned into a cubical.

All of a sudden you feel suffocated. You go to the kitchen to prop up the window only to face a solid wall that replaced the kitchen. You walk back to the couch with heavy steps. You reach the couch much quicker this time.

You start taking long strides alongside it, contemplating what to do now. You go back to the wall which originally had the door to the outside world and start pushing at each and every brick that you can touch.

Not a single brick gives in. You do the same to the wall facing the couch. You notice that the television set is not there anymore. You could swear you saw it when you came in.

As you reach midway, you notice that not a single wall has the paintings you had hung up. Each wall is painted in one color, each a different one. This does not look like your room at all. And you are stuck in it.

You resume pressing the wall but to no avail. Just then, you can feel the couch on your feet. You turn back only to discover, to your horror, the wall behind the couch, which was once a few meters away from the couch, is now right before you.

Not just that wall, but the other two walls too have ascended and entrapped you. There is no room for you to turn around now. You keep pushing the walls with your two hands, but some force, evidently stronger than you, is pushing the walls closer.

You feel the ceiling touch your head, the floor pushing into it, crushing your joints, completely destroying your body. Was this determined to happen? What if you had walked straight ahead instead of entering the room?

What if you had taken the right turn in the first place? Would this all have happened? Unfortunately, you are too broken to answer these questions. Only a heart, beating against the confining walls of your room.
