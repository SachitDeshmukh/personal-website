Moonlight

28 JUNE 2020 • Sachit Deshmukh • 10 min read
Suave

[adj.] charming, confident and elegant.

I stepped out into the balcony. The chilly air brushed past my skin, sending a chill down my spine. Shades of blue could be seen in the evening sky. A very light blue rim touched the horizon, fading into a deeper pigmented sky. A violet sheet covered the sky over my head. Studded on that sheet was a faintly shining crescent moon, smiling at me as it had been for all these years. I soon felt the presence of a human body standing beside me. There was a warm aura around it, almost soothing. Both of us were quietly enjoying the dusk. Breathing in sync, staring at the beauty before us.

“How beautifully does the moon smile at us,” I broke the silence, “it is a very soothing smile. It makes me feel very calm, you know,” I couldn't stop looking at the bright crescent moon, “I feel as though it talks to me. Every night. Every time I step out into my balcony just to look at it, with affection in my eyes. I seldom wish to go back to the warmth of the people I love, fearing that I might miss this calm.”

“I remember my dad doing that.” My gaze shifted towards the person standing beside me. “He used to take a small walk in this balcony, without fail, after every dinner. Why he ever did that, I could never tell. When I asked him, he would only say “You will get it one-day Amogh, one day you will”. The child in me kept wondering what it must be to go out there and see an object so far away as if it was just so close. I used to envy him, for being able to share his feelings with that full bright moon. I went after him one night out of curiosity, tried to narrate what I did in my day, and became a part of every night from then on,” Amogh looked at me, gave me a smile, and then our eyes went back to devour the void-like sky before our eyes. The mat black sky brought out the tiny glistening stars.

“I simply love it out here. I have shared so many things with the night,” I said looking at the patient moon, gleaming with happiness, blinking at us in harmony with the stars. “It knows every single day I spent at my school. It knows my crushes from high school. It has seen me being angry with my boyfriend,” my cheeks almost blushed. I refrained from looking at Amogh. He moved his hand towards me and I let his fingers entangle into mine. “It has gone through my breakups,” I said as I tightened the grip. “It has helped me bear the agony of some truths I didn't want to know.” Amogh chuckled, and for a moment I thought it was a reaction to what I said. But it turned out to be a reaction to a thought that crossed his mind.

“It knew I was gay before I had the slightest of an idea. It accepted me for who I was. It helped me go through the entire coming out journey. We worked out strategies, scrapped a few plans, doubted a few decisions, but we never gave up.”

“It was there with me when I started to like you.” I heard a faint laugh pass his mouth. “I used to wait for the night to come, eager to tell this companion of mine, how I felt when I met you that day,” I lifted his hand and gave the back of his palm a soft kiss. I held his hand close to my chest for some time. A sense of warmth took over me. I felt even closer to Amogh.

“It helped me rehearse how I would ask you out, Ninaad, how I would propose to you,” he brushed his thumb on the backside of my palm.

“I remember the night you asked me out,” I returned the gesture. A strong desire to hug developed. A noticeable tension grew between us as the urge became more prominent. “I could not stop talking about it all night. The stars seemed brighter that night, as though sending love from far away. It was a new moon night. And all I had was these stars. Stars in their formations, teasing my brain, making my finger trace every star out there.”

“That was one hell of a night. I was nervous you would say ‘no'. That we would never have a first date. And on the day of the date, before I could even utter a word, I broke out in a cold sweat. I was worried you would not like me.”

“You don't know how nervous I was. I didn't want to mess anything up. I wanted everything to run smoothly. I wanted to fall in love with you, and I wanted you to as well.” Our hands broke loose and went around each other back. I casually rested my head on his shoulder. He gave my chest a light stroke with his palm and kissed my head.

“I remember you were wearing a purple plaid shirt and denim trousers. I could not take my eyes off you. I believe you had shaved your beard, just to make sure I liked you, and I did indeed fall for it,” I could see him smile at me, exposing his beautiful teeth, and a soft dimple on the left cheek.

“I believe you were wearing a Cuban collar shirt having baby pink and white stripes. It looked so good on you. You were the first person I had met who could pull that off. That shirt paired with the khaki-colored dockers made you look so suave.” We were now facing each other, my hands were around his waist, his on my cheeks, recalling all the times we had spent together in the past entire year, all the moments that got us closer to each other, made us fall in love all over again.

“I do not want to lose you,” it just came out of my mouth.

“I too do not wish to lose you, not to any stupid thing, not to any crazy fight, not to a petty reason.”

“I want to keep you, forever. I want you to know how much I adore you.”

“I love you more, Ninu.”

We started to hug each other, softly swaying to the sounds of the crickets. With closed eyes and a wide smile on our face, we were savoring the moment. The warming touch of his body around mine, his strong arms around my body, how perfect everything felt was giving me assurance, was making me feel safe.

“We have been through so much in the past year,” he mumbled in my ear, gently caressing my back, “and something makes me want to stay.”

“The stupid fights over an alarm clock.”

“The debate about whether to buy a television set for the bedroom.”

“Which movie to stream on Netflix.”

“Choosing each other an outfit for a friend's wedding.”

“Cooking dinner together.”

“Confessing to our friends.”

“The candlelight dinner when the lights went out.”

“The time we went to Goa by car.”

“And we hugged each other at the beach, with the waves gently brushing past our feet.”

“I wished that moment never ended, I just wanted to remain in your arms.”

“And then when we got home and made love.” We both chuckled.

“The time we baked a cake for your birthday. What a mess we made of the kitchen!” I could not stop myself from laughing.

“Oh my god! I had so much fun that day!” I managed to utter. “The best part was how good the cake tasted in spite of the mess.”

“The time we almost planned a surprise party for your birthday. That kid next door, Ankush, had he not ruined it.”

“He is one hell of a kid. I enjoyed the party never-the-less. Especially the night after that,” he said in the naughtiest tone I had heard. We both smiled at the remark.

I could not love this night more. Everything was perfect. The air was pleasant, the moon was bright, the crickets were singing, and I was embracing the person I had loved with all my heart. Submitting my body onto his made me feel good about myself. I had someone who I could rely on, who I could fight with, who I could crack jokes with. And someone who had stayed for such a long time. We almost let go of the hug, and our cheeks brushed past the other's, our hands still wrapped around each other's waist. I looked into his eyes, filled with love and compassion. I could see his eyes were on mine too, piercing through my soul, shattering me from within. And somehow it felt correct.

“Do you remember the first time we kissed?”

“As though it happened yesterday.”

“We had been to my uncle's farmhouse.”

“And all we did was lie on our back in the fields.”

“Watching the red hot sun set.”

“And the dark colors of the night blend into the orange.”

“We were constantly talking about how we should probably join our friends back in the farmhouse.”

“And yet we never planned to go in.”

“And right when we were about to go back in,”

“I stumbled on the grass and before we knew it,”

“You were in my arms.”

“Looking at each other, as the tension grew, as the desire turned stronger.”

“And there we had it.”

As though living the whole moment, we kissed yet again. Every little thing we had cherished together was felt in this one touch.

“Under the moonlight, amongst the stars.”

“Just like the first time.” Our faces bore the biggest smiles possible.

“Happy Anniversary!” we both greeted, shared the tightest of hugs, and wished for it to never end, that I stay in his arms, in love with him, forever.

“We better get back to the kitchen, we got dishes to do,” he said, as we ended the hug. I just simply shrugged, still lost in the night, still smiling at the person I love, just like the moon smiling at us.
