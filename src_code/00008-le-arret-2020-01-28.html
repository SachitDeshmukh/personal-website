L' Arrêt

28 JANUARY 2020 • Sachit Deshmukh • 10 min read
Ruse

/ru:z/ [n.] an action intended to deceive or trick someone.

I place my backpack down and rest my body on the trunk of the tree. My tired head just looks up at the sky, regretting the very situation we are in. My friends, Ayden and Carter, and I are lost in a forest, which lies just behind our school. We took no map with us, our mobile devices are out of charge, and needless to say - none of us knows the way back. Tired and thirsty, our brains have stopped to work. Resting our bodies on the trunk of the tree, all of us raise our heads in hopes to see the skies. We can't tell if it is day or noon, the network of leaves blocks most of the light. It is almost pitch black near the ground. None of us have the energy to climb a tree and check if we are still living the same day, on which we left the school campus to have fun. We stay in the darkness, maintaining a silence not broken by a falling leaf. We have some water, wipe the sweat off our foreheads, pack our gears and are ready to set off again, in search of a way back home.

I take a nylon thread roll, unwind a few rounds, and wrap one end to the trunk of the tree (something that I should have done from the start). And so we start walking in a random direction, which seems to have been walked on before. The sound of our shoes pressing the fallen leaves is the only thing we hear. The nylon thread admires every passing tree, making us feel safer than before. Every step we take, we are reminded of the stories this forest is famous for. Some say there are wild animals, constantly roaming around. Some say there are spirits making noises. Some say the forest senses people approaching, and traps them inside. Many who entered the forest are known to not have ever returned.

One time a staff member had gone in to just get some flowers. No one saw him that day, and the next, and so for about twelve months, when finally he somehow managed to find his way back. That time, neither was he recognizable nor was he in one piece. From that day, the forest is blocked from general visiting, and no one is allowed to enter the forest alone.

Just then, we see a source of light coming through the trees. Without hesitation, we rush to the opening. Fresh air breezes past us. The sunlight makes our faces glow. We hear the birds chirp and the leaves rustle, creating a symphony. The only music we hear on this journey. While taking the fresh air in, my foot slips on a stone, and we realize that there is no further way past this point. Before I lose my balance and fall into the valley, Ayden grabs my hand and pulls me up. The valley's depths are unfathomable due to the dense fog. Not wanting to waste any time, we immediately turn back.

“There's a way we totally missed due to the sunlight,” says Carter, breaking the silence we were maintaining. We rearrange the nylon thread and start walking in the direction of that road. Patterns of shadows caused due to the sunlight behind us, amuse us. The only thing that makes us feel a bit better. The path gets progressively darker as if we were entering some sort of a cave. We near the spot where it seems to be the darkest. Not trusting the darkness, we stand still where we last stepped. I try to get a battery from the huge backpack I am carrying. The moment I switch it on, a treacherous trench unfolds in front of our eyes, and at that very moment, our bones turn into jelly.

“How deep must it be?” I ask.

“Who knows. But sure is scary.” Ayden says, his face most certainly showing the fear.

“Have you heard of Uncle Ben's story.” Cart adds in.

“Everyone has. Do you have to mention it right at this moment?” I try to avoid the topic. We start walking back towards the source of the sunlight.

“They say he fell into a pit as huge as this. And the horrors he faced were worse than hell!” he continues.

“Mere lies.” I do not want him to continue.

“That's exactly what people say, before seeing a photo of his once he returned after an entire year of torture.” he does not stop.

“Have you seen the photo?” Ayden asks, genuinely concerned.

“Not yet,” says Carter with a sinister smile on his face. “But I managed to bring a copy of it with me right now.”

Ayden curses the decision. “Why in the world would you do that?”

“Oh come on!” I try to make Ayden think rationally. “Do you believe in such lies?”

“Wait for me to show the photo to you guys.” Carter still wants to scare us with his stupid stories.

“I would give a hard pass on that.” I don't understand why do these rumors amuse him. “I think we are back on track. I see the bright sunlight.” We arrive at the same spot, with more knowledge of the forest. Having visited the two dead ends, we start walking on the only path left.

“Uncle Ben must have been on this very path,” Carter starts again. “It feels so cool to go on that very road.”

“How does it feel good?” questions Ayden. He is not very fond of these horror stories.

“Can't we just focus on walking?” I try to bring back the attention to walking. “The road does not seem to end soon.”

Carter keeps on talking about Uncle Ben. “When he entered the forest, he was fully clothed. When he came back though, his accessories were missing. He was seen barefoot on the campus when he finally arrived. It's weird that it took him twelve months to find the way back. My math teacher told me that Uncle Ben had been to hell and returned. There was a pocket journal in his pants. It had monthly entries. The entries were not in a language he understands, nor could he write. After deciphering, the incidents he explained were found to be no less than hell itself. One of them described his experience with a leopard. One attack, one pounce, one strike, and he lost his eye. That eye flew off to a nearby water body, making a splashing sound. Either the eye became bait to the alligators in the water body, or kept floating at the center of the same.”

“I smell water, guys!” I exclaim.

“Do you smell the eye in the water body?” asks Carter purposely.

“Shut up, focus,” I say. “There's definitely a water body nearby.”

We start exploring the surroundings. The sap on the trees gives a certain odor which keeps distracting me from the smell of the water. But I follow the path which seems to be walked on, carefully letting the nylon unroll. A sweet smell of something very pure teases our noses. And our bodies instinctively start moving towards the source. In a few more steps, the pure blue water emerges out of the land, embodying chunks of earth on which one can walk. Carter and I start to drink the water from the lake in a jiffy. But Ayden notices something in the middle of the lake as if all the chunks were pointing towards it. He carefully places his feet on the rugged surface of the chunks. Initially, he is not able to maintain balance, but as the chunks approach the core, he gets more efficient at it. Step by step, he gets faster at this. Soon he is able to see the other half of the lake, the green dense forests beyond our reach. Last few steps and he will be there. Maybe there is a way home from that side of the forest. “Could Uncle Ben have visited the other side?” he asks himself, as in two steps he would reach the core. One. Two. An eye emerges from the waters. Round. Bloody. Staring into the eyes of the viewer, the eye was of a miserable human.

“What did you find?” I ask.

He is dumbstruck, his face motionless. He turns around to block out the image of that horrendous eye. I wave at him to come back as quickly as possible. He starts walking on those chunks, his feet unstable. At every step he feels that the ground is shaking, is going to break and suck his whole body into the waters. He does not dare to take swift strides. Just then, the corner of one piece of land starts to rise, revealing structured sharp bones underneath. Water dripping from the edges of the bones in streaks. First one, then another, then many more and soon each and every one of the pieces rises.

“They are alligators! They are A-L-L-I-G-A-T-O-R-S!” He shouts from the bottom of his heart. Afraid of making one move, he stays still on the one alligator that chose not to open her hungry mouth. I run to grab a boulder to distract the alligators.

“I am throwing this boulder in the waters. The moment I throw it, jump in the water and start swimming for your life!”

He simply nods in agreement. And I do throw the boulder in the lake, hoping for the alligators to shift their threatening gaze. One splash. All eyes still, still looking at the bait. Not one eye blinks. And in a snap, each pair hastily approaches the bait. I don't see how I can help him out now. He is desperately waiting for us to do something. And he does not have enough time to wait.

“What are we to do?” I ask Carter, hoping for him to have some solution.

“This is what the forest does to people who are not supposed to be here.” He states.

“This is not helping.”

“Nothing can. He was destined to be killed.” He keeps on asserting.

All the alligators are just ready to pounce on Ayden when the one alligator he is standing on thereupon goes underwater, and at once comes out, taking his whole body in her mouth. Screams are heard, a splashing sound follows, and another scream. His entire body goes under the water, which each alligator tries to claim. His body is taken to the depths of the lakes, where the victorious alligator munches on him. The last thing we see of him is the blood that mixes with the clear waters of the lake, paired with the red sky of the sunset. An eye emerges out of the waters as the sun is about to set. Round. Bloody.

“The forest has claimed its prey. Now we shall be safe,” sighs Carter.

I choose not to react. I remain silent, mourning for Ayden, but not for too long, lest the alligators sprint for us next. We keep on walking ahead on the road, which is now darker than before.

“Uncle Ben did not just lose his eye,” Carter resumes his talk.

“Do we have to?”

“The forest shall otherwise take advantage of us not knowing.”

“Go ahead,” I say out of obligation.

“When Uncle Ben returned, he had not come in one piece. He was holding one of his hands in the other. His lifeless hand was covered in mud and ashes, just like his body. But there was no other stain on it. His astonished eye, on the beaten face, gave such horror to a staff lady, that she never came to school again. She mentioned how he was begging for water, his throat dry due to some flames he had to face; his skin was burnt, destroying every trait of his.”

“What did the school do with Uncle Ben once he returned?”

“Nothing. They could not. He was never seen ever again. Some believe he went back to the forest, like returning to one's home. He became a part of the forest. Some say he must have died. They smell weird odors from some parts of the forests.”

“Was the staff lady brought back to school?”

“After the incident, she did not dare to. She described the incident once and ran away. No one has heard from her either.”

“I still do not understand one thing. If he was burnt while being in the forest, did no one notice any forest-fire at all?

“His journal attempts to answer this question. It was no forest-fire. Humans. It was a fire set by humans.”

“Humans?”

“No one can be sure. But it could jolly well be the forest spirit.”

I simply nod, not wanting to know any more. It gets darker than before. In a few more minutes, I won't be able to see Carter. Every sound of the leaf crunching makes us more aware of the dangerous trees accompanying us on our left and right. The winds are strong. The rustling sound is unbearable. The silence Carter and I are maintaining lets us listen to each and every leaf getting stepped on. In suspicion that I am picking up sounds that are not present, I stop for a second, carefully paying attention to every step Carter taking. Carter stops too. And my suspicion proves true, the leaves keep getting crunched. Our senses heighten. One wrong move, and we too, will be killed.

“The fores-”

“Shush…”

The leaves still make noise. Each step can be distinctly heard. Loud and clear. The steps are periodical. First a single step, then two, then again a single step, and then there's a pause. Another step, then two and another one and then suddenly it's four-no five -

“R-U-N!” we both scream, running for our lives; directionless, but keeping company. We cannot afford to be alone in this nightmare-like forest.

Running as wildly as the animals which chased us, we somehow manage to avoid them. We sit down wherever we were standing, and take our camping headlights out. God for once is in our favor, as the headlights work fine. We look around us. Just trees. That's all we can see. Trees reaching sky high, accompanied by plants as low as the ground can be. And everything, a shade of green, darker due to the unfolding night. When everything seems to be back to normal, we start to walk in some direction. Not quite far, we hear drums and shouts.

“Humans!”

We immediately increase our speed. This time we hope that we would find people and will be able to go back home. As we near the source of the sound, an enormous bonfire emerges near a rock. People circling it. We hide behind a bush, lowering the intensity of the headlights, not wanting to be spotted immediately. The people are weirdly dressed and do not seem like regular humans. A few of them banging drums, a few shouting and a few simply dancing. To the side of this huge fire, there's a smaller fire; above this fire, there's some creature hanging. Two people are constantly turning the stick that the creature is tied to. Charring the creature's skin at every turn. “Perhaps they are cooking it.” One more turn and it turns out that the creature is none other than a human.

“They are cannibals!” exclaims Carter.

“Shush..”

They all stop, patiently listening to the source of a foreign voice. We at once turn off the headlights. The silence gets to my ears - no drums, no taps, no whispers, not even a single leaf getting crunched. Afraid to even lift our feet, we just stay there, waiting for them to continue their celebrations. They wait for a few more seconds and restart dancing and shouting. We still wait there, letting the atmosphere build up. Once everyone is back in their routine, we get going. We try to sneak past the tribe's territory, carefully following trees - trunk to trunk. When we leave the drums and chants well behind us, we take normal steps. We turn on the headlights, not realizing that it was a mistake. My friend sets his foot in the wrong place and triggers an animal trap, which lifts him off the ground. He gets stuck, upside down. Before I can even think, an arrow is shot at his left hand that knocks him unconscious. The skin around his hand starts to turn bluish. Tranquilizers. Realizing I could be next, I sprint. Away from the trap, away from the cannibals, away from danger.

It is pitch black. The only thing I can see is what the headlight shines on. I keep running on the road, till I feel that I am quite far away. I rest my hand on a tree trunk, panting, gasping for air. My heavy breathes break the silence. I make no efforts to figure out where have I arrived. I try to take a steady, deep breath in. And exhale it in one go. Another one in, which I try to exhale gradually. One more in, and then out. I feel a bit calmer. I start exploring the surroundings. There does not seem any other way, but ahead. When I am just about to take a step forward, I notice a knot on the trunk of the tree I rested on. My eyes grow. It's made up of nylon too. The only difference - the color of the nylon is different. The tree trunk where the nylon is tied seems to be sawed. The nylon has been there for a while. I touch it to examine how old it is.

The moment I touch the nylon, the leaves of the tree rustle. I withdraw my hand, not wanting to face any trouble at this time of the night. Not wanting to go deeper into the forest, I decide to stay here till the sun rises. I take my backpack off and place it near the trunk of the tree. I sit down and rest my back on the tree. I look up at the trees, still unable to see the sky. “When would all this end?” I wonder. “What ruses are these to keep me away from my home?” Suddenly, a body falls down from the trees. Its one eye missing, left hand chopped. The clothes it has, are burnt. The body is bare feet. Hanging on this one branch of the tree, with a rope around its neck, it keeps staring at me, with a single, wide-open eye.
